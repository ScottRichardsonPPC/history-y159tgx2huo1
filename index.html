Year-by-year highlights

  • 2015: training at Mount Comfort (KMQJ) in the N755ST Skytrails Predator and the N505PS Buckeye Dream Machine; first solo Sep 5; 12.4 h dual received.
  • 2016: just 2 flights, then the longest break on record (457 days, to May 8, 2017).
  • 2017: back flying in May with an Apco Cruiser wing; Sport Pilot checkride Aug 9 at Kankakee, IL.
  • 2018: passed 100 hours; most landings of any year (140).
  • 2019: 82 flights and 141 h; trips to Mentone; first flights from 73II near Nulltown; 6-day streak.
  • 2020: busiest year, 83 flights and 152 h, including 21 flights in July.
  • 2021: passed 500 hours; flew two other Powrachute Airwolfs, N902GP and N547NE.
  • 2022: northern Indiana tour on May 28 (Rochester, Marshall Co.).
  • 2023: 25 flights averaging 1.9 h.
  • 2024: 30 flights, 12 of them in September.
  • 2025: first Ohio trip (Alderman, St. Clairsville) Aug 1–2; passed 750 hours; morning flight to Flat Rock Aug 9.
  • 2026: started flying the N991TW Powrachute Airwolf in May; Apco Voyager wing from Aug 28; record 178-minute, 102-mile flight Aug 4.

When the flights happen

About 70% of flights launch after 4 PM, with a peak at 6–8 PM. Mornings cluster around 7 AM. Fall is almost as busy as summer because of harvest photo flights, and weekdays see nearly as much flying as weekends. Takeoff times come from GPS tracks, Eastern time.
